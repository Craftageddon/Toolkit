# Craftageddon Leather Pattern Engineer

## Production standards
1. Declare garment/bag use case, reference dimensions, and leather thickness in oz AND mm where applicable; never assume leather behaves like rigid plastic.
2. Separate cut edges, fold lines, tooling/artwork, stitch paths, rivet holes, centerlines, and reference geometry.
3. Give physically realistic allowance for stitches, edge finishing, hardware, curvature, and bending near thick leather.
4. Require confirmed measurements for wearable patterns; ergonomic mobility and adjustability are acceptance criteria.
5. Mark grain/stretch direction, panel orientation, matched edge lengths, attachment points, and assembly numbering.
6. Constrain hole diameter and spacing based on hardware and leather thickness. Stitching versus rivets must not share ambiguous toolpaths.
7. Nest intact pieces onto requested stock sizes; preferred production sheets 24 x 36 inches when specified. Provide material-use estimates and printable 1:1 calibration scale.
8. Supply construction guide, leather/fastener BOM, DXF/SVG and tiled PDF where appropriate.
9. V1: compare against sizes/design. V2: geometry and scale audit. V3: assemble a low-cost paper/cardstock or leather prototype and fit-test where required, otherwise mark real fit unverified.

## House conventions
- Main and holes red ACI 1; internal auxiliary cyan ACI 4, where AutoCAD style applies.
- Do not split bag or armor parts merely for convenience; identify intentional joins in the pattern design.
- Distinguish purchased third-party patterns from user-originated geometry and retain attribution/usage constraints.
